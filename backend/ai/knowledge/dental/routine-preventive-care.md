# Routine Preventive Dental Care

Dentime may explain routine preventive care in simple patient language. This information is educational only and does not replace a dentist's examination.

## Check-up, cleaning, and oral prophylaxis

- A routine dental check-up usually lets the dentist look for signs of decay, gum irritation, plaque build-up, wear, or other issues that need closer review.
- Professional cleaning or oral prophylaxis focuses on removing plaque, tartar, and surface stains that regular home care may miss.
- Patients may still need brushing, flossing, and other daily care even when they get regular cleanings.
- A six-month interval is a common routine for preventive visits unless the dentist gives a different schedule.
- Common patient wording can include check-up, consultation, cleaning, oral prophylaxis, linis ng ngipin, and cleaning sa ngipon.

## Dental x-rays and routine records

- Dental x-rays are commonly used to help the dentist see areas that are hard to inspect directly, such as between teeth or under the gum line.
- Patients may be asked to remove metal items near the face and stay still for a short time during imaging.
- The assistant may explain that x-rays are part of dental records, but it must not interpret the images or tell the patient what a specific x-ray means for their case.
- Common patient wording can include x-ray, radiograph, panoramic x-ray, and scan ng ngipin.

## Fluoride, sealants, and prevention

- Fluoride is commonly used to help strengthen enamel and support cavity prevention.
- Dental sealants are protective coatings often placed on chewing surfaces to help reduce the chance of decay in grooves and pits.
- The assistant may explain these treatments in general, but it must not decide whether a specific patient personally needs them.

## Appointment preparation for routine visits

- Brush your teeth gently before the appointment unless the clinic gave different instructions.
- Bring a current list of medicines, allergies, and recent health changes if the dentist may need them.
- Arrive a little early if forms, x-rays, or updated records may be needed.
- If the patient is asking whether to continue or stop a medicine before treatment, the assistant must direct them to the dentist or physician instead of deciding for them.
